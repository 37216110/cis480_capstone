PWF | Integer (Target) | Binary label indicating power failure mode

### Synopsis
#### Transformation(s) Applied
<!-- scope: telescope -->
<!-- info: Select **all applicable** transformations
that were applied to the dataset. -->
- Cleaning Mismatched Values

Twenty-seven observations were identified in which the `Machine failure` field was inconsistent with the independent failure-mode indicators. According to the dataset documentation, a machine failure should be recorded when at least one of the individual failure modes is present. These 27 observations violated this relationship and were removed from the cleaned dataset.

**Cleaning Mismatched Values**

**Method:** A logical consistency check was performed between the overall `Machine failure` field and the five independent failure-mode indicators: `TWF`, `HDF`, `PWF`, `OSF`, and `RNF`. Records were flagged when the overall machine-failure label was inconsistent with the individual failure indicators. The 27 records identified by this validation check were removed rather than modifying their labels because the correct value could not be determined with sufficient confidence.

**Platforms, tools, or libraries:**
- Python: Used to perform the data validation and cleaning.
- pandas: Used to filter, identify, and remove inconsistent observations.
- Jupyter Notebook: Used to conduct and document the data-cleaning process.


**Transformation Results:** A total of **27 inconsistent observations were identified and removed**. After removal, the remaining records satisfy the expected logical relationship between `Machine failure` and the independent failure-mode indicators.


**Additional Notes:** Removing the observations was selected instead of changing the failure labels because it was not possible to determine whether the overall `Machine failure` field or one of the individual failure-mode fields contained the erroneous value.

#### Cleaning Mismatched Value(s)
<!-- scope: telescope -->
<!-- info: Which fields in the data were corrected
for mismatched values? -->
A consistency check identified **27 observations** in which the overall `Machine failure` indicator did not agree with the independent failure-mode indicators.

**Machine failure:** 27 observations involved inconsistent machine-failure labeling.

**TWF, HDF, PWF, OSF, and RNF:** These fields were evaluated collectively to determine whether the corresponding `Machine failure` value was logically consistent.

No values within these fields were manually corrected. The affected observations were removed from the dataset.

#### Method(s) Used
<!-- scope: periscope -->
<!-- info: How were incorrect or mismatched
values cleaned? What other choices
were considered? -->
Each observation was evaluated by comparing `Machine failure` with the five independent failure-mode fields. An observation was considered inconsistent when the overall machine-failure indicator did not correspond with the presence or absence of the individual failure-mode indicators.

Rather than changing one or more values based on an assumption about which field was incorrect, the entire inconsistent observation was removed. This prevents potentially incorrect labels from being introduced into the cleaned dataset.

Before cleaning, the dataset contained 27 observations that violated the documented relationship between the overall machine-failure label and the individual failure-mode indicators. After cleaning, these 27 observations were no longer present.

**Above:** Comparison of logical inconsistencies before and after removal of mismatched machine-failure observations.

**Additional Notes:** The transformation removed complete observations rather than modifying individual field values. This approach avoids introducing assumptions about which of the conflicting values was correct.

#### Residual & Other Risk(s)
<!-- scope: telescope -->
<!-- info: What risks were introduced because of
this transformation? Which risks were
mitigated? -->
**Data Loss Risk:** Removing 27 observations reduces the amount of data available for analysis and modeling. The reduction represents only **0.27% of the original 10,000 observations**, limiting the overall loss of data.

**Class Distribution Risk:** Because machine failures are less common than normal operating observations, removing records associated with failure indicators could disproportionately affect the minority failure class. Class distributions should therefore be reviewed before and after cleaning.

**Label Integrity Risk:** Retaining inconsistent records could introduce label noise, particularly if `Machine failure` is used as the target variable in predictive modeling. Removing the inconsistent records mitigates this risk.


#### Human Oversight Measure(s)
<!-- scope: periscope -->
<!-- info: What human oversight measures,
including additional testing,
investigations and approvals were
taken due to this transformation? -->
The 27 flagged observations were reviewed against the documented relationship between `Machine failure` and the independent failure-mode indicators. The cleaning decision was based on the dataset documentation stating that a process failure should result in the `Machine failure` label being set to 1 when at least one of the specified failure modes occurs.

After removal, the consistency check was repeated to verify that no observations remained with the identified mismatch.

#### Additional Considerations
<!-- scope: microscope -->
<!-- info: What additional considerations were made? -->
An alternative approach would have been to correct the `Machine failure` field based on the individual failure-mode indicators. However, this would assume that the individual indicators were correct and that the overall failure label was incorrect. Because the source of the inconsistency could not be independently verified, removal was considered more conservative than altering the original labels.

The effect of removing these records should also be considered when evaluating class balance, model performance, and failure rates because machine-failure observations represent a relatively small portion of the dataset.
